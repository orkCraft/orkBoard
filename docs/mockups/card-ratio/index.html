1 · One card, 5 : 7, read by its four corners

Every card is 5 : 7: 144 × 202 in the column, the hand, the lanes and the discard, 404 × 566 opened and as a new goal. Every card says the same thing in the same corner: top left the rune (which goal), top right the kind (what card this is: a feature, a bug, a question, a fork, a failure, done), bottom left who (whose work), bottom right how much (its cost before it is agreed, its time once at work, the time it took when done; one figure, never two). Between them the title, and what the place has room for. The signal colours (yellow: it waits for you; red: it failed) are in the kind's corner and the frame only. A card stands while it plays (at work, in your hand, opened); it lies on its side, face down, while it waits: being read (In analysis) or waiting to start (To review), a deck that deals itself onto the table. Face down it has no corners: its seal is its rune, its ring what it waits for.

2 · The Orkmaster's column

2.1 · Two piles lie face down; only what is at work stands

The column is 160 px (today 128). In analysis is a loose pile of backs, still being sorted: its front card's ring runs, and the goal being read is named under its title (§206.3). To review is a squared deck that deals itself onto the table: each back's ring fills in eighths as its start comes, a goal that waits for you has a still yellow ring and a yellow edge, and the next to start is named under its title with its time, so you can hold it before it starts. Pressed, a back opens its goal's spec. At work stands, 144 × 202: the name in up to four lines, what it does now, and its foot (who, how much), which is what the pile shows of each card behind its front one. The backs are 157 × 112, 13 px under the window's edge, so every pile keeps one right edge (§236). Hover a card to slide it out.

2.2 · It never scrolls: the piles give way to the window

Two piles of backs take 112 px each, whatever they hold, so only At work gives way, in three steps, never a scrollbar: 1, its cards behind show their foot (26 px: who and how much); 2, they become thin edges (3 px), the furthest first; 3, the pile sinks under the hand's rim, the way the hand's own cards go under the window's edge, the rim falling between two lines: its front card shows its top (rune, kind, name), the cards behind it a sliver at its right. Nothing counts what waits. Drag the height: 650 is a 1366 × 768 laptop's browser, 790 a 1440 × 900, 940 a 1920 × 1080.

3 · The hand

3.1 · Cut under the title; who asks is the lane

A hand card lies under the lane of who asks (hand.js: a party's stacks under its lane, the Orkmaster's under the column), so the card does not repeat it. A choice is made only when the card is lifted (a fork becomes a hearing, a question shows its ways), so its cost is shown there, at the moment of choosing; a cost that grew is a card of its own kind. So the window's edge cuts the card under its title, at 100 px: the rune, the kind, the question in three lines. The hand is 28 px lower than today and the table taller. Hovered, the card rises and shows why it is asked, who asks and what it costs.

3.2 · A goal asks you: the deck deals it to your hand

When a goal in To review asks you, its back (its ring still and yellow) is dealt to you: it lifts out of the deck, turns face up and stands on its way, and lands in the hand under the lane of the leader who asks, where the window's edge cuts it. Face up it has its four corners, its kind what it asks (a question). Under reduced motion it is in the hand at once.

4 · Opened, and a new goal

The opened card is 404 × 566 and never grows: its body scrolls inside, the answer stays above its foot. Its corners are the hand card's, larger; the bottom right is the cost of the way picked and changes as you pick (try it). A new goal's card is the same card with its corners empty and dashed, each saying what will stand there; they fill as the goal is made (pick a kind and see the top right fill). It teaches the corners before the first goal is laid.

5 · Phone

The phone's hand card is 80 × 112, cut at 62 under its title. On a phone the hand has no lanes, so who asks is a head between the groups: the hand is laid by leader, a small head before each leader's cards. Pressed, a card rises and shows the rest; tapped, it opens. The crown's piles are a screen of their own, a list that may scroll. The opened card is 5 : 7 at the screen's width; while the keyboard is up it keeps its top corners and gives up the rest of its shape.

The kind's corner speaks two vocabularies: a goal's (feature, bug, tidy, screen) in the column, the lanes and the discard; a decision's (question, fork, failure, hypothesis, cost grown) in the hand and opened. The kind changes as the card moves, its place never.

How much is one figure, by the card's state: before it is agreed its cost (the gem), at work its time left, done the time it took, waiting for you the words asks you.
