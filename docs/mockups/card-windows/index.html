1 · The Orkmaster's column: no window

The column says where a goal is, and its pile, its rune, its name and its foot say it already. Today half of every upright card is an empty window. Without it the card is its rune and its whole name (three lines), and its foot: the wait as a bar, the time, the leaders' heads at work. A pile shows more cards in the same height, and the face-down cards of In analysis keep their seal (they have nothing else).

2 · The hand: the kind in the corner, who asks beside it

The hand says what is asked of you and by whom. The kind is a sign in the card's corner (a question, a fork, it failed, a hypothesis due, a cost grown), in its signal's colour; who asks is a small head beside it, never lost (a member asks only its leader, and the hand says which leader). The title gets three whole lines: no title cut in the middle of a line. The card is about two thirds of today's height, so the hand is lower and the table taller.

3 · The leaders' lanes: a window onto the work

Here the window keeps its place and shows the work itself, never a picture for its own sake (the one look: the races' heads the only pictures): who will take it while it waits, its steps while it is at work (§207), what it made when it is done or checked, by its craft, and a failure's reason. All of it is read from what the Board already has (git, the steps, the files the work made, the gate's checks, the hypotheses), with no call to a model.

4 · The whole window, proposed

The column of short cards, the lanes of tall ones, the hand of short ones under them. The cards keep one face (frame, header, label row), so they still read as one deck: the height says where the work is. The hand is about as tall as the potions and the die now set it.
