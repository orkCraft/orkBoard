1 · The inventory: a merchant's stall

The backpack opens a stall on the table, as a deck-builder's merchant lays one out: the Orkmaster stands at the rug's left and says what it would take; the wares lie on the rug, every one at once, a price plate under each; your hand is what you carry of the chair picked. On a leader's chair: its heroes on the rug's top shelf (a set each: school, flow, size, how much it decides itself, how often it says no), the ways of the card you lift from your hand on the shelf below, its seats (its AI tool and its tier) as the small things at the right. A price is what a ware adds to the first potion's week (the vial and +2%; a saving in green; money said as money, $2/night); what you carry reads in your hand. Hover a ware: your card shows it, the potions what it costs, the merchant's face how dear it is. Press it: it flies into your hand over the card it replaces, with Undo. The merchant's own service is Back to default, free: drop a card of your hand on it (or on the discard). The chairs say whose, the potions and the die are pressed as they are, Stop all is the door.

Shut the backpack and press a chair: its table is laid as its hero reads it (section 2). Take a hero on the rug, shut the backpack, and the same chair's table is laid the new hero's way.

2 · The eighteen tables: each leader's table, as its hero reads it

A hero brings its school's frame (decisions §266.3, §184): the same craft's map (spec 95 §3), laid by its school. The sixteen heroes on the rug, four a craft for Product, Design and Tech, two for Run and Marketing; and the two that are not traded: the Orkmaster, whose table is the Board, and Release Night King, whose table is dev against main. Each on its craft's texture; colour only where something calls (an alert, a failure, a pass, a goal's element). The project, its goals and its numbers are made up. Their screenshots: shots/tables/; the stall's: shots/stall-*.png.

3 · Whose table: a texture a chair, a stitch for the inventory

No colour: a pattern in the ink at a few percent on the table's surface, under the cards, so whose table it is reads at a glance on any day, not only in the inventory. The inventory is told by its edge, a stitch like the backpack's seam: the mode by the edge, whose by the texture, so the two never mix.

4 · The heroes' faces

Four heroes a leader's race (two for Run's and Marketing's), each its race's elder head with its skin or helm, its horns, antlers or wings and one detail changed (heads/make_heads.py); the hood stays the tier's gear, the unicorn's helm a warm metal, no skin the action's green; the moods laid over each from orkUI's own. At the window's sizes: 48 × 44 and 24 × 22.

What it changes

A stall, not a sheet. Everything a chair can take lies on the rug at once with its price, as at a merchant's: nothing to open to learn what there is. The Orkmaster is the merchant: it lays the wares and stars its pick.

The rug and the hand turned round, open for the owner. §266.1 lays what is set on the table and the ways in the hand. The stall lays the ways on the rug (the table) and what you carry in the hand, as a merchant's does. If this is kept, a new decision replaces §266.1's table and hand; the rest of §266 stands.

A price you can read. The plate says the share of the first potion's week (+2%, a saving in green) and money as money ($2/night); the hover says the rest. No gold, no coins to collect: the potions are the purse.

Back to default is the merchant's service, free, at its feet; the discard keeps taking a card too (§266.1).

A hero lays its table. Its school is a frame the table is laid by: C4's containers and hotspots, DDD's contexts, XP's red and green, an opportunity tree, a funnel, a trace. Eighteen tables, one map a craft (spec 95 §3), its hero's way.

Yours outlives the hero. A hero brings its own ways of answering its party's questions; what you set yourself is marked yours and stays when the hero changes. Your trust is yours (§52.6).

Open for the owner. Do Run's and Marketing's tables (an SLO's budget, a trace, a funnel, a store page) become maps of their own in spec 95 §3? Does the hero's table replace the craft's map, or lay over it as a lens?

An experiment of 6–7 November 2026 (the stall and the eighteen tables, 7 November), not a goal and not a spec.
