# orkUI / office / runes

**The runes**: twelve marks on a **5 x 7** grid, orkUI's own (orkBoard's decisions §185). They spell nothing and are read by no one, and none is a
letter of a real alphabet: no arrow, no Y on a stem, no lightning, no diamond on legs (`tests/test_orkui.py` keeps a list of shapes no rune may
take). They are the table's one vocabulary of magic: the portal's ring and the + card's seal of summoning are written in them, so a goal is
summoned at the + and leaves through a ring in the same runes.

**Five of the twelve are the five elements** (0.20.0, orkBoard's decisions §186): a goal's element is its rune, so the mark on a card and the rune on the
table are one picture. **Seven are the decoration**, and the ring and the seal are written in those alone; each keeps a place at its top for the goal's
element, drawn by the page in the element's own colour, so the table says, in runes, *this goal*.

    elements (runes.json)   meadow tusks    ice key    dust comb    void eye    lava wave
    decoration              hook, ladder, spiral, staff, hourglass, diamond, knot      (the ring and the seal, in this order, from 45 degrees)

`tools/build_runes.py` refuses a ring or a seal that carries an element's rune, and writes `../elements/<element>.svg` (see below).

    runes.json         the source: the twelve runes, which five are the elements, and the geometry of the ring and the seal (python tools/build_runes.py writes the rest)
    <name>.svg         key, eye, hook, tusks, ladder, spiral, staff, hourglass, diamond, comb, knot, wave: 5 x 7
    ../elements/       the five elements, each its rune centred in 7 x 7 (generated by the same tool)
    ring-dots.svg      the portal's ring: a dotted circle with a gap under each rune and the place      96 x 96
    ring-runes.svg     the portal's ring: seven runes upright on it, at 45 degrees and on, clockwise   96 x 96
    sigil-lines.svg    the seal: two circles, a star of two squares, eight ticks                        68 x 68
    sigil-runes.svg    the seal: seven runes in the band between its circles, at 45 degrees and on      68 x 68
    sigil-small.svg    the seal at an 80 px card: a circle, a diamond, four ticks, no runes             36 x 36

Every file is a grid of unit squares, one `path` (`M{x} {y}h1v1h-1z` for every pixel, row by row from the top), `fill="#000"`,
`shape-rendering="crispEdges"`: masks with no colour of their own, like the signs. The lines are the cells whose centre lies on them, so the
seal's lines keep the eight symmetries of its centre; every number rounds half up. A rune is changed in `runes.json`, then
`python tools/build_runes.py` (`--check` says whether the files are what the source makes), in the table of `tests/test_orkui.py` and in
orkBoard's decisions together, never in a face.

## A rune

    <span class="ork-rune key" aria-hidden="true"></span>          10 x 14 (2 px a cell)
    <span class="ork-rune knot big" aria-hidden="true"></span>     20 x 28 (4 px a cell)

`.ork-rune.<name>` is drawn in the colour of its text. A face draws one only where it means nothing (an accent), never as a word. An element's rune is drawn
by `.ork-elem` in the element's colour (`../elements/README.md`), not by `.ork-rune`.

## The portal's ring: the portal's place

`.ork-portal-ring` is the portal's place on the table, laid before there is anything to send: **192 x 192** (96 x 96 cells at 2 px), one size
(the narrow width draws no ring). The portal goes inside it:

    <span class="ork-portal-ring" aria-hidden="true"></span>                                                          asleep
    <span class="ork-portal-ring is-open"><span class="ork-portal" aria-hidden="true"></span></span>                  open
    <span class="ork-portal-ring is-sealed"><span class="ork-portal is-sealed" aria-hidden="true"></span></span>      sealed
    <span class="ork-portal-ring is-open"><span class="ork-portal-ring__seal"><span class="ork-elem dust" aria-hidden="true"></span></span>
      <span class="ork-portal" aria-hidden="true"></span></span>                                                   open, with a goal's element in its place

- **Asleep** (no class): the dots in the edge, the runes in the faint ink, nothing moves and nothing has colour. Nothing to send.
- **`is-open`**: the portal is in it (a goal can be played). The runes are `--ork-portal`, and the dots go under the portal's own ring.
- **`is-taking`** with `is-open`: the runes in `--ork-portal-hi` while a card is held over it (give the portal `is-taking` too).
- **`is-sealed`**: the portal with its X, and the runes grey again. Asleep has no portal and sealed has one, so they never read alike.
- **`is-waking`** with `is-open`: the opening, played once. The runes light one after another, a quick step each (`--ork-motion-quick`, eight
  steps), the dots go, then the portal's eye opens in five steps. Give the portal `is-new` for its shimmer. Take the class off to play it again.
- **The place** (0.20.0): the top of the ring, position 0, is where a goal's element lies. No rune is drawn there and the dots keep as clear of its 7 x 7 box
  as of a rune. `.ork-portal-ring__seal` is a **14 x 14** box (`position: absolute; z-index: 2`) at the top left of that box, at **90 px, 6 px** (cell 45, 3 at 2 px a
  cell; `tools/build_runes.py` `ring_place`, and a test keeps the CSS and the generator together). The caller puts the element in it, drawn in its own colour at full
  strength: `<span class="ork-portal-ring__seal"><span class="ork-elem dust" aria-hidden="true"></span></span>`. Empty it draws nothing; asleep the caller leaves it
  empty. `.is-sealed` draws the element in the faint ink, as `.dim` does, and `.is-open.is-waking` shows it with the portal's eye (the same delay, five steps).
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion` nothing turns: a waking ring is open at once, with its element.

## The sigil: the + card's seal of summoning

`.ork-sigil`: `<span class="ork-sigil" aria-hidden="true"></span>` inside `.ork-card.ork-plus`, before its icon: two circles, a band of seven runes and a star of
two squares, **136 x 136** (68 x 68 cells at 2 px), centred under the +. It takes the card's text colour (`currentColor`): the lines at about
a quarter of its strength, the runes a little more, so the + stays the one bright thing. On the card's hover or focus the lines are a step
brighter, and the runes light once, one after another clockwise from the top, in eight steps (`--ork-motion-play` and `-base`), a sector of 45 degrees
centred on each of the eight positions in turn (the top, the goal's place, lights nothing), then rest a little brighter. **`.small`** is the seal at an 80 px
card, **72 x 72** (36 x 36 cells), with a circle, a diamond and four ticks, no runes and no place. Under reduced motion the runes do not turn: when hovered,
they are just brighter. A 180 px card keeps the 136 px seal.

**The place** (0.20.0): the top, position 0, is where the goal's element lies. `.ork-sigil__seal` is a **14 x 14** box (`position: absolute; z-index: 2`) at the top left of
the 7 x 7 box there, at **62 px, 12 px** (cell 31, 6 at 2 px a cell; `tools/build_runes.py` `sigil_place`, kept together with the CSS by a test). It holds
`<span class="ork-elem lava" aria-hidden="true"></span>` in the element's own colour at full strength; it takes no part in the sweep. Empty it draws nothing;
`.ork-sigil.small` has no seal. The geometry (outer circle 31, inner 19, ticks 33, runes on the band's middle at radius 25) keeps every rune two cells from every
line, a 7 x 7 box at the top three, and the middle 13 cells clear for the +.

## A card's place

`.ork-card-place`: `<span class="ork-card-place" aria-hidden="true"></span>` marks where a pile will lie (the discard before its first card): a card's outline in pixel
dashes (2 px by 4) in the edge at under half its strength, with no fill, at 5 : 7. The page gives it its width. It cannot be pressed and it never moves.
